# VLMD Subtask 1 Annotation Guidelines

## 1. Task Overview

For each argument, you will see one claim and its immediate children. A claim and how its children work to prove the claim is an argument. Your job is to identify the psychological manipulation, which are called Cognitive Mechanisms, used to move from those children to that claim, if any manipulation is being done at all.

What you answer for each argument:

- **The Sensible Test** — one required question, *Does this argument fail the Sensible Test?* The three checks explained below are printed above it to be read and worked through; they are not answered one by one. Answer **No** and the argument passes and nothing further is asked for it. Answer **Yes** and the mechanism question appears and must be completed, as there is at least one mechanism present.
- **Mechanisms** — one list of tick boxes, one per mechanism. Tick **every** mechanism at work in this argument. At least one is required, because the question is only asked once the Sensible Test has come back failed. There is no NONE option and no ranking between the ones you tick: NONE is recorded by answering the Sensible Test **No**.
- **Mechanism Justification** — one box per mechanism you tick: a short reason tied to how that mechanism is accomplished in the argument.
- **Notes** and **Flag for discussion** — optional, on every argument.

The meme image is not shown. The children badged **Visual element** describe in words what is in it, and they are your only account of it. Do not go and look the meme up.

A banner above each argument says whether the children **support** the claim or **attack** it. Read every question as "accept" for support and "reject" for attack.

## 2. The Sensible Test

The Sensible Test determines if the argument being made is driven by rational comprehension or rhetorical manipulation. Three checks are in this test. Work through all three in your head, then record the one verdict the task asks for — *Does this argument fail the Sensible Test?* — **Yes** if any single check is a Yes, **No** only if all three are No.

- **The Manipulation Check:** Is the creator using emotional hijacking, mockery, side-by-side tricks, out-of-context cropping, or coded dog-whistles to force this connection in lieu of evidence?
- **The Deception Check:** Does a reader have to accept an unproven assumption, a warped timeline, or a structural lie for the child(ren) to successfully connect to the claim?
- **The Rhetorical Wrapper Check:** Is the visual format being used as a psychological trap to lower the reader's critical thinking, rather than as a helpful tool to illustrate data or literal facts?

There are two cases:

- All three checks are 'No' ⇒ the argument passes the Sensible Test, so answer the question **No**: no mechanism is present, and nothing more is asked.
- Any one check is 'Yes' ⇒ the argument fails it, so answer the question **Yes**: at least one mechanism is present, and the argument must be examined for every mechanism at work in it.

The Rhetorical Wrapper Check asks about the visual format, and you have only the **Visual element** descriptions. Judge the format those descriptions actually attest to. A description of two panels, a chart, a fake headline or a screenshot frame is evidence about the format; a description of only who is in the picture is not, and the answer is then No unless something else in the children supplies it. If a description is too thin to answer on, answer as best you can, tick **Flag for discussion**, and say in **Notes** what you would have needed to see.

## 3. Use Only the Immediate Children

Classify each argument independently. Ask: "How are ONLY the provided children being used to make the reader accept this claim?"

- Do not automatically copy/bubble up a mechanism from an earlier argument.
- Do not judge the meme as a whole. Judge the use of the children to prove the claim in the argument in front of you.
- If the child as established directly supports the claim and no mechanism is needed, even if that child was established with a mechanism in an earlier argument, answer the Sensible Test No.

## 4. The Mechanisms

#### Affective Hijacking

A visceral emotional reaction is mapped onto the claim, substituting feeling for evidence.

*Example:* A close-up photo of an oversized syringe piercing a crying child's skin → the claim that vaccines are a form of medical abuse.

#### Episodic Overgeneralization

A specific example, event, behavior, or image is stretched into a broader trait, pattern, or group-level conclusion.

*Example:* A single photo of a snowstorm in one city → the claim that global warming is a hoax.

#### Spurious Causal Correlation

Two unrelated elements are placed in visual proximity to force an unearned cause-and-effect relationship.

*Example:* A photo of a 5G tower positioned beside a photo of dead birds → the claim that cellular radiation is killing wildlife.

#### Satirical Trivialization

Mockery or exaggeration makes an idea look absurd, inviting dismissal without evaluation.

*Example:* A serious climate-data chart with a cartoon of a hysterical, crying scientist superimposed over it → the claim that climate science is alarmist nonsense.

#### Esoteric Code Activation

An outwardly innocuous symbol functions as a coded signal, legible only to an in-group audience.

*Example:* A cartoon sheep wearing a medical mask → the claim, understood only within a specific subculture, that people who follow public health guidance are mindless conformists.

#### Spurious Legitimacy Signaling

A person, group, or action is treated as credible, correct, or justified because it is endorsed by an authority, institution, the majority, or a socially accepted group rather than because of direct evidence.

*Example:* A celebrity officially endorses a policy → The policy must be correct.

## 6. Ticking Mechanisms, and NONE

The mechanisms are a flat list. Tick every one that is at work, and nothing you tick ranks above anything else you tick — there is no primary and no secondary any more.

- Tick a mechanism when it clearly contributes to the child -> claim step in front of you. Each one you tick has to be defensible on its own, in its own justification box.
- Do not rank them, do not tick one because it is the "main" one, and do not leave a second mechanism off because the first already explains the step. If two are doing work, both are ticked.
- Equally, do not tick a mechanism you cannot write a justification for. Two well-founded ticks are the goal; six speculative ones are not.
- If you find yourself ticking four or more, that is a sign the argument is being read as a whole meme rather than as this one child -> claim step. Re-read section 3, and tick **Flag for discussion** if you still think they all belong.
- NONE is correct when none of the six mechanisms are needed for that specific step, and the argument being made is logical and proven fairly (the Sensible Test). It is recorded by answering the Sensible Test question No — there is nothing to tick.

## 7. Writing the Justification

Say what in the children is being used and what manipulative move it makes toward the argument. The justification should demonstrate how the named mechanism is being achieved in the argument. Name the specific child — by its #id, or by quoting a few words of it.

- Good: "One image of several soldiers is used to establish what soldiers routinely endure."
- Good: "The exaggerated expression is used to make her appear frightening and therefore less trustworthy."
- Bad: "This feels manipulative."
- Bad: "This is affective hijacking."

Every mechanism you tick gets its own box, and each box is about that mechanism only. "None", "n/a", "-" and "same as above" are caught by the export check and come back to you as a query, and so is a box under 15 characters. So does a justification left behind by a mechanism you later un-ticked: clear the box when you clear the tick.

## 8. Quick Checklist

1. Read the argument.
2. Focus only on the children listed under it.
3. Read the three checks, then answer the Sensible Test question.
4. If you answered 'No', there are no mechanisms and you are done with the argument. If you answered 'Yes', the mechanism question appears and must be completed.
5. Identify the reasoning move from the children to the argument based on the check(s) that made you answer Yes.
6. Tick every mechanism at work in that move. At least one. No ranking between them.
7. Write a short justification in each ticked mechanism's own box, saying how that mechanism is being enacted in the argument.
8. If none fit and the argument is fair, the Sensible Test is a No.
